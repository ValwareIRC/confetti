Confetti · redesign · five directions

Five ways this could look

The first attempt borrowed ircuitry's cream chrome but left its colour behind, so everything landed in the same beige register — five identical white bars where an oper should be instantly distinguishable from a security-group. These five all fix that, and all do the things the first one couldn't: add a block, move a block, group blocks into categories you define, and work across every .conf in the folder rather than one file at a time.

Every file, as a treeEverything under conf/ and its subfolders — help/, aliases/ — not one file at a time. Point at unrealircd/ or at conf/ and it finds them either way.
Identity at a glanceBlock type readable without reading. Colour on the whole card, not a 17px glyph.
Add · move · reorderNew blocks, dragging between categories, dragging between files.
Your own categoriesName, colour, icon, description. Collapse the whole group to one tile.

The bar is that Syzop should want to use it. That rules out anything that reads as a toy — so where these get playful, it's in warmth and colour rather than in pretending config isn't config. All five keep the real directive names visible and the file itself one keystroke away.

01

Board

bold · drag-first

Columns are your categories. Cards are blocks. You drag a card to another column to recategorise it, or onto a file chip at the top to move it to that file. The thing you asked for most — moving blocks around — is the whole metaphor rather than a feature bolted on.

Identity

Category colour fills the column head; the card carries the real directive name in mono as its loudest element, with key values as pills. You read class clients · sendq 200k without opening anything.

Moving things

Drag between columns to recategorise, drag onto a file chip to move files. Shown mid-drag above: set anti-flood heading for network.conf.

The tree

Left rail, always there, with per-file block counts. Greyed rows are files with nothing in them yet or non-config folders like tls/.

What it costs

A column shows one category, so a whole config never fits on screen at once. Deep nesting (set::anti-flood::everyone) has to open in a panel — the card can only summarise.

02

Folio

cozy, far bolder

The current app with its volume turned all the way up. Files become physical folder tabs. Categories become bands with a coloured spine, an icon and your description. Blocks become substantial cards with a saturated icon and their two or three most telling values — not a row of grey text.

Identity

A saturated icon tile plus a coloured cap on every card, and the plain name at 13px bold with the real directive underneath. The three most telling values are always on the face of the card.

Moving things

Drag a card to another band to recategorise, or onto a folder tab to move it to that file. Bands fold to a single strip.

The tree

Full depth, with help/ and aliases/ expanded and counts per file. Tabs across the top are the files you're actually working in.

What it costs

The least dense of the five — three cards a row means a big config scrolls a long way. It's the closest to what's already built, so also the least of a leap.

03

Console

expert · dense · dark

Built for the person who already knows what ceppp means. One line per block, everything visible at once, colour as thin semantic rails rather than decoration, and a command bar that takes set::anti-flood and takes you there. This is the one Syzop would open twice.

Identity

A 3px colour rail plus the directive name in a distinct hue. Type and label sit in fixed columns, so the eye runs straight down them — no hunting.

Moving things

Keyboard-first: ⌥N for a new block, drag a line onto a file in the tree, or type the destination in the command bar. Categories are the group headers.

The tree

Full depth with counts, and every block line carries its file:line — so the tree tells you where things live and the list tells you where each one came from.

What it costs

It stops being ircuitry's sibling. Dark and dense is a different world from cozy pastel — the warmth would live in the writing, not the surface.

04

Atlas

navigation-first · calm

A map of the whole configuration is permanently on the left — every file, every block, as small coloured chips. You never lose your place across four files, and moving a block is dragging its chip into a different file's group. The right side is one thing at a time, given proper room.

Identity

Colour is the only thing carrying block type in the map, and it does all the work — you see at a glance that spamfilter.conf is entirely anti-abuse pink and opers.conf entirely lilac.

Moving things

Drag a chip from one file's group into another's. That is literally moving the block between files, and the map is the only place you need to look.

The tree

The tree is the map — folders collapse into one row when you're not using them, and each file shows what's inside rather than just a name.

What it costs

One block at a time on the right, so comparing two class blocks means clicking between them. The map is abstract until you learn the colours.

05

Workbench

playful · spatial

Blocks are objects on a pegboard and you arrange them where you like. Categories are regions you draw around a group — give them a colour, an icon, a description, and fold the whole region into a single tile. Files are trays along the bottom; drop a module into a tray to move it there. The most game-like, and the one with the most rope to hang itself with.

Identity

Every module wears its type as a coloured header bar with the real directive name in it, and references between blocks show as little ports — listen saying it uses class clients.

Moving things

Drag anywhere. Into a region to categorise, into a tray to change file. Regions fold to a tile, shown right: Aliases, 9 blocks, folded.

The tree

The trays along the bottom are the tree, flattened to drop targets. Folders become trays too. A full tree stays available for files you're not currently arranging.

What it costs

Someone has to do the arranging, and a 27-block config needs a "tidy up" button to stay sane. It's also the one most at risk of reading as unserious to an expert.

Worth saying

These aren't exclusive. Console's density and Folio's warmth could be one app with a density switch — the same data, two skins. And whichever wins, the folder discovery, the add/move/reorder commands and the user-defined categories are the same work underneath; only the surface differs. If two of these appeal for different reasons, say which parts and I'll draw the hybrid rather than making you pick a whole one.
